Before this program, I wasn't sure what leadership looked like in practice. I've been fortunate to work with supportive managers, and I've also learned that when I truly believe in something, I will advocate for it-even when it's uncomfortable.

I wanted this to feel like a journey of discovery, so I wrote it in the same sequence as the program days.

Some days I took away a lot, and other days I took away very little-sometimes because of the content, and sometimes because of where my head was at.

I like to come away with tools I can apply quickly. I also know I won't remember everything, so I'm capturing what I want to practice on purpose.

We were encouraged to make this plan our own, so I'm going to build something that fits how I work: a simple leadership app that turns these situations into prompts I can reuse.

# How to use this plan

Use each Leadership Situation as a quick checklist: pick the situation that matches what you're facing, answer the prompts, and capture one concrete next step to try.

The app concept is simply a way to reuse these prompts over time, track what you tried, and review what worked so you keep practicing on purpose.

## Leadership Situations

Situation 1: You have a decision to make, and you and a team member disagree.

- Write down why you believe your decision is the right one (if possible).
- What experiences and biases do you have about this situation?
- Do your past experiences still apply here, or do they need re-evaluation?
- Ask the team member why they feel this is the right decision; what was their answer?
- Ask the team member what experience they are bringing into this situation that you could learn from.
- Is there a 3<sup>rd</sup> party that can help with this situation? If so, reach out to them.

Situation 2: I'm in a meeting (not leading) and I want to stay present.

- What is this meeting about?
- Who is at the meeting and why are they there?
- What are you bringing to the meeting?
- What do you hope to get from the meeting?
- How will you ensure you capture what others share in this meeting? Are you taking notes? Is someone else (or an AI) taking minutes?
- How will you know when to speak and when to listen?
- How and when will you review the meeting?

Situation 3: I'm leading a meeting and I want to stay present.

- Related situation 2.
- What do you expect from everyone else in the meeting, are they aware of this?
- Is there an agenda for this meeting, have you shared it with everyone attending?

## Leadership Situations

Situation 4: Your project is not going as planned.

- What are your expectations of where this project should be in terms of time and cost?
- How would you describe its current state?
- What is the biggest driver of its current state being behind the goal?
- What can you do to help with this driver? Don't be afraid to put down ideas that might not work.
- What can you do to help make sure this project doesn't fail?
- Take a step back, can this project still make its timeline if blockers are removed?
- Are there other parts of this project that are going well or ahead of schedule?
- If this project can't be completed on schedule, what can be done to get acceptance of why it should continue? Failure is always an option. Is this project still a good idea, or is this just an issue of sunk costs?
- What is the critical path for your project? Can resources be used in different ways to achieve the same goal?

## Leadership Situations

Situation 5: I'm forming a new project team, and I want to ensure communication and collaboration.

- How will this project be run? Not every project has to run the same. For example, if the project is a potluck, then you could A) Have a list of food categories such as vegetable, protein, dessert; You could ask each person to say what they are bringing and track it in a sign-up sheet. Or B) you could just get everyone to bring something.
- How do each of your team members expect this project to be done? If you are not on the same page, you should discuss this.
- Is there a deadline or is there a goal. Which one is more important?
- How do you like to communicate? Email, kanban board, instant messaging, in person meetings, online meetings, anything else.
- How often do you want to communicate? This may vary by situation-for example, you might want issues raised immediately by instant message or a quick call, while routine status updates may not need a message at all.
- How does each member of the team want to communicate? Name each member of the team and some examples of how they want to communicate.
- How can you adjust your style to meet your team?
- Why should your team adjust to your style?

Situation 6: I want to learn my team's values so I can use them to guide my decisions.

- Take a first pass on what you think the team values are; these are useful so there isn't the pressure to come up with examples or ideas on the spot. Examples could be Communication or Working together; you will need to flesh these out more.
- Book a time for a meeting of at least an hour but possibly 2, in person, to meet and discuss the topic.
- Introduce the idea of team values "Things that guide the decisions we make as a team and that we inspire to live up. These are also a good reference point when bringing decisions up the organization structure"
- Start with one of the values you had prepared and write it down. Debate it as a team.
- Go around the team and ask if they have any that they would like to suggest. If any come up as part of other discussions, write them down.
- Setup meetings regularly depending on the pace of your business to review these values. Ask questions like "Have we been honouring this value? Is this value still relevant to us"

## Leadership Situations

Situation 7: I need to have a difficult conversation with my team or with a member of my team.

- Before you go into this meeting you should prepare your version of events. What happened that prompted this conversation?
- What do you see at the key facts of the situation? Make sure you separate the facts from the interpretations.
- What feelings are you bringing into this conversation?
- How are you going to stay curious and not defensive in this conversation?
- What is the pace of the conversation? Do you have time to have the conversation, or will you be rushing it? If you don't have time to slow down and listen, then you should wait.
- How will you know when the conversation is over? What do you expect as an outcome?
- How will you measure that outcome? is there a follow up needed to make sure you are all supporting each other?

This was a very lively presentation, and the big takeaway was the magnets.

## Leadership Situations

Situation 8: I want to understand what the emotional magnets of my coworkers are so I can appeal to what energizes them.

- How can I run an exercise to learn this? Start by reviewing the emotional magnets and sharing them with your team.
- We are all energized by different things. These are called emotional magnets-core drivers that influence how we make decisions, what motivates us, and what drains us. This exercise will help us understand our own magnets and learn how to work more effectively with each other.
- Safety: People motivated by Safety are energized by security, structure, and self‑preservation, making careful, sensible decisions to minimize risk and protect themselves and those they care about.
- Achievement: People motivated by Achievement are energized by challenge and success, driven to set and accomplish goals, perform at a high level, and receive recognition for their efforts.
- Value: People motivated by Value are energized by worth, efficiency, and long‑term impact, focusing on smart use of resources, return on investment, and creating lasting significance from their efforts.
- Experience: People motivated by Experience are energized by curiosity and growth, thriving on learning, change, creativity, and exploring new possibilities that move them forward.
- Before the meeting have each person write down their primary and secondary magnets and what they think their team members magnets are.
- In the meeting go around each team member and have each person say what they think the magnets for other person are, discuss as needed.
- Reference book: Emotional Magnetism: How to Communicate to Ignite Connection in Your Relationships by Sandy Gerber

This one for me is super important and tightly connected to a lot of the work I do.

## Leadership Situations

Situation 9: I have a meeting and I want to go in without bias.

- Remember that we will all have bias, the best you can do is be aware of them.
- When you think about the person or people you are meeting with what have you decided about them already that is negative.
- Where does this negative bias come from and does it apply to this situation?
- When you think about the person or people you are meeting with what have you decided about them already that is positive. Thinking critically can require you to think about the good and the bad.
- Where does this positive bias come from and does it apply to this situation?
- Think before you respond in the meeting, are your responses based on one of these biases? How would you react to a different person?

Situation 10: I want to make sure I'm being fair and considering disabilities. What do I need to remember?

- In Canada 27% of Canadians aged 15 years and older have a disability. Many of them are not visible.
- Accessibility helps even those that don't need it. Try these writing tips:
- Plain language
- Clear headings in the correct order
- Descriptive link text if writing for the web
- Inclusive and respectful language.
- Accurate captions and transcripts for audiovisual content such as videos
- If you are trying to accommodate one person and unsure you can always ask them and they can help you out.

# Appendix A: App description

This space is for me to scribble down descriptions and notes I want for the app.

The application will be written in dotnet 10 asp.net and will run on a docker compose. It will connect to a SQL server in the same docker compose. For development it will connect to a SQL server hosted on a remote machine.

The user will have the option to browse without logging in but will need to log in to create.

Its login will be either phone number or email address based. Accounts will not have a password but will send either an email or text message through azure communications services with a 6-digit one time code to login, this code is single use and should be all numbers and only good for 1 hour or 10 bad attempts at the code. The codes will be valid for one hour. The user session will be saved for one month in a cookie and will be refreshed every time they use the web app. They will be asked for a name the first time they log in. This will only be used when sharing situations.

The next screen is the main menu. It has several options. The first option is "What leadership situation can I help you with?"

At the bottom of the main menu, it will say. "Nothing you do in this app will be shared unless you share it. "

The user will be presented with a list of community situations that are listed by situation short description and author name. They will also see in this list their own private situations and community situations that they have used or saved for later. This list will be searchable.

The order will be:

- Used: sort by (1) userUseCount descending, then (2) lastUsedAt descending, then (3) situationTitle ascending (A-Z) as a tie-breaker.
- Situations they have saved ordered by how many users have used or saved the situation.
- Situations that other users have shared to the community ordered by the number of users who have used that situation.

There is also a button to see the newest community situations that will list community situations that the user has not saved or used, ordered newest to oldest, and shows the date they were added.

Once they have selected a situation it will show the actions they can take.

Actions have the form of prompt and are either a checkbox that you can mark as done or provide long text box to provide your answer.

If the user wants to work through the exercise, they can click the button "I'm having this situation" which will create a private instance of the situation called an experience. They can also save the situation to their list of saved situations.

Each instance of a situation is called an experience. It should be saved with the following information.

- Date
- Provide details about your experience. This is for you when you review the situation
- A list of each action and either button that says "I've done this" or a text area to fill out. Changes should be saved on focus loss or every minute. There should also be a button to mark the ones with text as done. When saving if there is a change record the date and time and show it. Users can remove an action from this experience or all future experiences of the same situation. They can also remove it from the template for others if they created the template.
- Did this help with your situation?
- Add your own action for this situation

Each situation will have zero or more related situation. The user can add all actions from the related situation to the instance.

Add Action:

- Prompt: A text area for them to provide a prompt in markdown format.
- Does this action want a text response?
- Share publicly with the community.
- Save and return

The creator of an action should have the ability to edit the action.
